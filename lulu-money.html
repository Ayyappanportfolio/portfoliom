    01Project context

    A live product, with people depending on it

    LuLu Money is powered by LuLu Exchange — a real financial services company with over 200 branches worldwide, moving money to more than 170 countries. This wasn't a concept redesign. Real customers depended on it to get money to their families, and it had to keep working while I rebuilt it underneath them.

    Business context

    The 2018 hybrid app covered one product — account-to-account transfer. The business goal was a digital bank: forex, bill payments, wallets, wallet transfers.

    Constraints

    Six markets at once — UAE, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Bahrain and APAC — each with its own regulations and language expectations.

    Who uses it

    Blue-collar workers, often on shared Android devices, to white-collar professionals managing investments — one app, very different comfort levels.

    Delivery

    Four sprints — discover, define, ideate, design — with development and testing running alongside the later sprints.

    02Problem discovery

    I refused to start with "the UI needs modernizing"

    A rebuild this size fails first at the business-alignment layer, not the interface layer. So the research plan opened with stakeholders across six markets before a single end-user session.

    Assumption I rejected

    "The interface looks old, so modernizing it will fix the app."

    What was already known

    A single-product 2018 app, a business ambition to become a digital bank, and six live markets.

    Research plan

    • Stakeholders first — UAE, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Bahrain and the wider APAC business, to find the strategic constraint.
    • Personas next — to pin down who actually sends money, and how comfortable they are with digital tools.
    • 321 store reviews — so customers' own words could confirm or challenge what stakeholders believed.
    • Competitor teardown — Wise and Al Ansari Exchange, to see where they were strong and where they fell short.
    Evidence gap

    Formal hypotheses and participant recruitment criteria for the end-user research weren't documented, so none are shown here rather than reconstructed after the fact.

    03User research

    What stakeholders, personas and 321 reviews said

    Roughly eight hours across three sessions with stakeholders surfaced the real constraint immediately:

    “It's not customer centric — only account transfer, no western-money-like services. There's demand for more products.”Stakeholder interview

    Stakeholders were also clear on who uses the app: migrant workers, blue and white collar, sending money home regularly to support families, education and investments — many on Android with limited technical proficiency, most comfortable in WhatsApp, Facebook and YouTube rather than banking apps.

    In customers' own words

    Against Wise and Al Ansari Exchange the pattern held: competitors had useful features but buried them in confusing hierarchies, or shipped sign-up flows that broke on login.

    Research limitations

    The original stakeholder interview guide and full session transcripts weren't preserved beyond these themes. No quantitative survey ran alongside the interviews and review mining, and reviews skew toward people motivated to complain.

    Need

    To understand every screen, in their own language.

    Pain point

    Not knowing whether a transfer worked.

    Opportunity

    Make status, fees and language visible before a decision, not after.

    06Personas and segments

    Two people, three segments — and no one-size user

    Primary

    Md Shahid Alam

    A construction worker sending cash home on a tight schedule, with low tolerance for friction.

    Secondary

    Amit Sharma

    A white-collar professional, more comfortable with digital tools but just as unforgiving of a broken experience.

    Beyond the two personas I grouped the customer base honestly, because a rebuild aimed at everyone serves no one:

    The lapsed

    See LuLu Money as old-fashioned and moved to Liv or Western Union.

    The current

    Loyal and passively engaged customers using it today.

    The future

    People who would consider LuLu Money when the need arises.

    Journey mapping not produced

    A stage-by-stage journey map with tracked emotions wasn't made for this rebuild. The send-money flow in section 09 is where the critical moments were identified and designed for directly.

    07Problem definition

    “Not customer centric” is a headline, not a problem

    Keeping symptom, problem and root cause apart stopped the team treating bad reviews as a visual-polish task.

    Symptom

    Negative reviews citing crashes, missing Arabic support and a broken OTP flow.

    ≠
    Problem

    A single-product 2018 app serving a multi-product, multi-market, multi-literacy user base.

    ≠
    Root cause

    The app was never re-architected as the business grew from remittance-only to a digital-bank ambition.

    How might we

    How might we rebuild LuLu Money into a digital-bank foundation without breaking the trust migrant workers already place in it to get money home reliably?

    08Opportunities and priorities

    Crucial, important, not really — tap any problem

    Every flagged problem was sorted into three tiers rather than treated as urgent. Select one to see where it landed and what it became.

    Evidence gap

    The three tiers are documented; a numeric impact-versus-effort score for each item is not, so no scores are invented. The strategic idea that ran through every solution was to use LuLu's actual advantage over digital-only rivals — its agent network — bridging online payment with offline cash pickup. Divergent brainstorming and rejected concepts weren't documented separately.

    10Wireframes to final design

    One flow for a construction worker and an office professional

    Designing one flow for two personas didn't mean averaging them. It meant finding the one moment — fee transparency — that mattered to both, for the same reason. Wireframes were plain first and argued about before visual polish arrived.

    Designing for six markets at once

    Language

    Language selection surfaced at onboarding and in settings, directly answering the Arabic-language complaint.

    Regulation

    Compliance and localization treated as core requirements from sprint one, not edge cases to patch later.

    11Validation

    The real test was six markets, not a usability lab

    12Outcome and learnings

    What I'd tell the next lead

    What improved: product breadth, transaction visibility and language coverage were addressed by design. What remains unresolved: no formal usability round confirmed these changes reduced the complaints seen in reviews. What's next: a real post-launch review audit, the moderated round above, then forex booking, bill payments and the wallet experience as first-class products.

    Start with stakeholders when the risk is strategic

    User interviews would have found UI problems. Stakeholder interviews found a business-model problem hiding behind them.

    Segment before you personalize

    The lapsed, the current and the future customer needed different things from the same redesign.

    A market rollout isn't a usability test

    Six markets proved the product worked. They didn't prove the design decisions were right — that gap is worth naming.

    Regulation and language are UX constraints

    Designing for six markets at once meant treating compliance and localization as core requirements from sprint one.

    The through-line: rebuilding a live financial product isn't about designing the ideal version from scratch. It's knowing which parts of the legacy experience were trust, not just friction, and protecting those while you fix everything else.
