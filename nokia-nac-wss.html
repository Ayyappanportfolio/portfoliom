Product Context

What is Nokia NAC · WSS?

Nokia NAC (Network Admission Control) and WSS (Wireless Security Suite) are enterprise-grade products that control which devices can join a corporate network and enforce wireless security policies across thousands of IoT endpoints.

Network Admission Control
Authenticates and authorises every device before it joins the network. Enforces certificate-based identity, posture checks, and policy assignments automatically.
Wireless Security Suite
Manages SSID-level security rules, rogue AP detection, encryption policies, and scheduled enforcement windows for enterprise wireless networks.
IoT Device Management
Unified inventory, health monitoring, and lifecycle management for thousands of heterogeneous IoT endpoints — cameras, sensors, access points, and industrial devices.
Problem Discovery

What was breaking for users

Initial stakeholder interviews and field observations with Nokia enterprise clients revealed three compounding pain areas that were costing teams hours each week.

Critical
Fragmented onboarding
Admins navigated 4+ separate consoles to enrol a single IoT device — no unified flow, no progress state. Average time: 35 minutes per device.
High
Invisible security posture
No real-time consolidated view of wireless endpoint health. Policy violations went undetected for 2–6 hours, discovered only during scheduled audits.
High
CLI-only policy authoring
Security rules required raw CLI configuration. A misconfigured rule during a shift handover caused 62% of access-failure tickets.

Wireframes

Three rounds of iteration

We ran three lo-fi wireframe rounds, each tested with 8 participants. Key iterations addressed mental model mismatches uncovered in testing.

Round 1 → Issues found
Check-in flow had 12 steps — users lost context mid-wizard
Dashboard showed raw numbers without trend or status colour
Policy editor required knowing SSID names beforehand
Incident list had no clear severity hierarchy
Round 3 → Resolved
Wizard reduced to 5 focused steps with persistent progress state
Dashboard now leads with compliance % + colour-coded health ring
SSID picker replaced text input with dropdown populated from inventory
Events list uses severity chips + visual weight to guide attention

Reflection

What I learned

Enterprise ≠ tolerant of complexity
A common assumption is that power-users accept dense, complex UIs. Our research showed the opposite — admins wanted simplicity precisely because their environment was already complex. Every step we removed was compounding relief.
Card sort changed the architecture significantly
Our first IA was product-logic-driven (matching the backend modules). Card sort showed users think in terms of tasks. Shipping the second IA was the single biggest usability lever in this project.
The field technician persona was underserved
Initial scope focused on admin and security users. Shadowing field technicians revealed they were the entry point for most device onboarding — and the most poorly served. Adding their perspective reshaped the Check-In wizard entirely.
Auto-suggestions reduce errors more than validation
We added input validation everywhere in Round 1. What actually reduced errors was surfacing smart defaults — like auto-assigning policy groups based on device type. Preventing mistakes is more effective than catching them.
