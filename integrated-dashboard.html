Integrated Products Dashboard — Making M2P's Partner Ecosystem Legible

"The Integrated Products Dashboard consolidates financial metrics and KPIs for M2P's internal teams and external partners. Its purpose is to make business performance easier to assess, investigate and communicate" — the brief, as documented. This page follows the real design file's own section sequence, not a generic template.

Ayyappan Rajendiran UX Designer, M2P Fintech
Fig 01 — Cover Page Section, as documented in the source design file

Project Context

M2P runs core banking infrastructure for banks and fintechs, who in turn serve corporates, who in turn serve end customers — a chain four layers deep. As M2P rolled out more future-ready, customer-relevant banking services, the gaps got harder to ignore: reduced visibility, delayed issue identification, limited transparency, and real difficulty tracking whether a partner's program was healthy.

Stakeholders

M2P's Business Development, Product, Client Engagement & Delivery, Operations, and API Analyst functions — plus external partner interviews with Transcorp, Walrus, Muvin, YBL, Equitas, and TVS Credit.

Live tenants

The product's own tenant selector lists real partners on the platform, including TcSwipe, Early Salary, Muvin, and Walrus.

Existing ecosystem

Partners compiled MIS manually and routed requests over email; M2P's own teams tracked client health the same way, through report generation and manual analysis.

My role

UX Designer — research across internal and external stakeholders, synthesis, IA, and the shipped design.

Stage 01
Problem Statement

Visibility that depended on someone else generating a report

As documented in the source file

"Reduced visibility and limited transparency made key metrics difficult to track. Issues could be identified late, while understanding product performance depended on report generation and manual analysis."

Monitor the business

See performance without waiting on a generated report.

Explain performance

Give BD a grounded answer when a partner asks why.

Support partner goals

Let partners track and guide their own program.

The design challenge

How might we turn scattered financial information into an accessible explanation? Make the right scope visible, expose meaningful signals beyond totals, and connect monitoring with deeper diagnosis and reporting.

Empathy Mapping

A cross-role reporting and diagnosis lens

Built from documented findings — not direct observation, and not an empathy map of one interviewed individual. Thinks and Feels below are inferred hypotheses to test, not measured results.

Says (documented quote)

  • "Analytics and statistics should be easily accessible, and tailored to the goals of clients."

Does (reported practices)

  • Generates reports to find transaction status.
  • Requests data in email threads.
  • Processes analytics manually.

Thinks (inferred hypothesis)

  • May question whether the scope or period is right, what changed, and whether a reason can be explained.

Feels (inferred hypothesis)

  • Uncertain when information is fragmented; dependent on others.
  • More confident when context and trends are clear.
Center need: "Data to be projected at both tenant and corporate level. Should be able to see the data of the entire business or an individual business over a preferred time period." — labeled Reporting & diagnosis.
Evidence gap

The emotional response here is a question for evaluation, not a finding — "Make context persistent, make interpretation possible" is the design direction it points toward.

Stage 07
User Flow

Scope, monitor, diagnose, share — with branches for when data isn't there

"Proposed from the original design intention. The main path connects scope, monitoring, diagnosis and sharing. Access, unavailable-data and unresolved-cause branches are recommendations, not implemented or validated claims."

Open dashboard→ Access permitted?→ Choose tenant / corporate / period→ Data available?→ Assess scoped metrics
Identify decline segment / reason→ Drill into detail→ Cause resolved?→ Prepare explanation→ Share / export
  • No access: request access or contact the administrator, then retry.
  • No data available: adjust scope or retry data retrieval — loops back to tenant/corporate/period selection.
  • Cause not resolved: retain evidence and escalate the unknown cause, rather than forcing a false explanation.
Stage 08
Card Sorting

A standalone proposed open-sort study

"Dashboard users group shuffled domain labels in ways that make sense to them, then name their own groups." Proposed to include internal BD/Product, Operations, and partner stakeholders — asking each to group the shuffled labels, create group names, and explain ambiguous placements, without exposing the designer's hierarchy first.

ReversalsKYCIncomeActive cardsDecline reasonsCustomersCard expiryTransaction countReportsNetworkMerchant categoriesUnallotted cardsSuccessCorporate volumeExpenses

Proposed prompt: "Group these labels as you would expect to find them in the dashboard. Name your groups. Keep a label separate or place it differently if that better fits your task."

Designer hypothesis (illustrative grouping): Transactions → success, decline reasons, reversals, network, merchant categories. Customers → activity and KYC. Cards → allotted/unallotted, activity, and expiry. Reports → generate/share. Income/expenses → financial overview.

What this does not claim

No completed sorting or tree testing is evidenced. There are no participant groups, agreement rates, or results here — this is a proposed validation study, intended to challenge the hypothesis above before refining the architecture, including a proposed tree test for "find a decline reason," "check KYC," "locate expiring cards," and "generate a scoped report."

Stage 09
Information Architecture

From overview into transactions, customers, cards, and reporting

"The architecture moves from an overview into transactions, customers/KYC, cards and reporting. Tenant/corporate scope, period and network/business filters give the detail a consistent context; sharing and export support handoff."

Transactions

Success · Declines · Reversals, each broken down by channel, network, and business.

Customers / KYC

Active · Inactive, by customer range and KYC conversion.

Cards

Allotted · Unallotted, by activity and issued/expiry.

Reports

Report type and period, with share and export output status.

Within Transactions, Success and Decline each break further into volume, channel, merchant, and reason — then split again by view-by-corporate and view-by-MCC, since a partner and an M2P operator read the same breakdown from opposite directions. Customers and Cards follow the same shape: a shared top-level filter set, then a network view and a business view of the identical dataset.

Proposed validation — keep the hidden detail discoverable

Check whether users find "Others" and know what the top-five view excludes. Validate keyboard and touch alternatives to hover, as well as domain terminology and paths, with task-based tree testing.

Stage 11
Visual Design

The original final dashboard, published exactly as designed

The finished dashboard leans on three ideas traceable directly to research: a hierarchical structure with filters visible at every level, infographics that let people stay at their preferred zoom level (trend line or raw number), and segmentation that lets the same dataset be read by network, business, or customer range. The four screens below are the original final dashboard, not a recreation.

Evidence gap

These are reported aggregates, not recalculated counts — the denominator and rounding relative to the 22 people are unknown. "Little difficulty" was defined in the source as independent completion with missed metrics, which conflicts with the results wording "little help." The rubric and raw records would need recovery before treating this as precise task success. These are usability signals, not launch impact.

Proposed validation — task protocol and evidence sheet

Evaluate the investigation, not just the interface

Use a controlled scenario with a known tenant/corporate, period, expected data, and allowed recipients. Observe an unassisted attempt first; log every prompt separately; include wrong scope, unavailable data, and unresolved-cause as planned recovery conditions.

R01 — Select the assigned tenant/corporate + period.
Correct selected scope; wrong-scope errors and recovery. To collect, not historical results: role + task outcome, elapsed time, errors, prompts, recovery, confidence and interpretation notes.
R02 — Explain whether declines rose against the comparison period.
Correct direction, units, and reference period; interpretation confidence.
R03 — Identify a decline segment/reason; state what is and isn't proven.
Correct segment; unsupported causal claims; need for additional evidence.
R04 — Generate/share the assigned report to permitted recipients.
Correct report scope and recipients; report failure/retry recovery.

Proposed outcome rubric: independent (correct outcome, no task-direction help) · assisted (correct outcome after a logged prompt or intervention) · failed (required outcome not reached, or an incorrect/unsafe final output) — recording partial progress and critical errors separately, not calling a missed metric complete. Per-role coverage: assess R01–R04 for BD & Product, Operations, and external partners within permitted scope; no sample counts are proposed here ahead of agreeing recruitment and coverage.

Reflection + next steps

A clearer path from business context to defensible decisions

The strongest story is a traceable task, not an unsupported savings claim. The honest next step is to recover ownership and study records, establish a baseline, validate scope/permissions, and run the task protocol above — then revise requirements, design, and retest. Numeric targets, post-launch adoption, time/cost, and financial results are unavailable.

Future roadmap — possibilities only

Real-time refresh, advanced modelling, AI/ML, mobile, and SharePoint integration are optional future directions documented in the source file — not delivered capabilities or evidence of impact.

Key Learnings

Interview both ends of the relationship
M2P's internal teams and its external partners named the same gap independently — that's what confirmed it was structural, not a one-off complaint.
User groups beat a single persona for an ecosystem product
Three named groups — BD & Product, Operations, and external partners — kept every design trade-off honest about which audience it actually served.
Prioritize per audience, not once for everyone
The same transaction data ranked differently depending on whether a partner or an M2P operator was asking — the prioritisation matrix had to be run twice.
An instrument isn't the same thing as a result
The 60/25/15/0 split is a real, documented signal — but recovering the rubric and raw per-task records matters more than repeating the headline number.

The through-line: a dashboard serving an entire ecosystem isn't one product with one user. It's several products wearing the same interface, entered from different directions — consumer research, business research, and partner research each surfaced a different half of the same gap.
