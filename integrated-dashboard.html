One Dashboard for M2P's Entire Partner Ecosystem

M2P's banks, fintechs, and corporates were emailing spreadsheets to understand their own transaction data. The full trace — including the one project here with real, scored usability results.

Ayyappan Rajendiran UX Designer, M2P Fintech
Fig 01 — The dashboard, the problem statement, and the process that got there

Project Context

M2P runs core banking infrastructure for banks and fintechs, who in turn serve corporates, who in turn serve end customers — a chain four layers deep. As M2P rolled out more future-ready, customer-relevant banking services, the gaps got harder to ignore: reduced visibility, delayed issue identification, limited transparency, and real difficulty tracking whether a partner's program was healthy.

Stakeholders

M2P's Business Development, Product, Client Engagement, Operations, and API Analysis teams — plus external partners including Transcorp, Walrus, Muvin, YBL, Equitas, and TVS Credit.

Existing ecosystem

Partners uploaded reports into tools like Power BI manually; M2P's own teams tracked client health over email threads.

Constraints

One interface had to genuinely serve three audiences with different jobs, not just different logins.

My role

UX Designer — research across both internal and external stakeholders, IA, and the shipped design.

Stage 01
Empathise

Two very different audiences, describing the same gap

Problem Discovery

Interviewing both ends of the same relationship

The assumption to test: that "reduced visibility" meant the same thing to M2P's own teams and to the partners depending on M2P's infrastructure. I didn't assume it did — I interviewed both sides separately.

User Research

Internal stakeholders and named external partners

Internally, I spoke with people across Business Development, Products, Client Engagement & Delivery, Operations, and API Analysis. What came back wasn't a feature request — it was a description of information poverty:

Information is wealth. The biggest challenge today is I don't have information on what my client is doing.— M2P stakeholder interview

Then real partners actually using M2P's rails — Transcorp, Walrus, Muvin, YBL, Equitas, and TVS Credit. Their challenges were operationally specific: getting an MIS on a daily basis was difficult, tracking transaction reversals was hard, fetching customer statistics was a manual chore, and some partners had hired additional resources purely to fetch and process reports by hand.

Problem Definition

From email threads to a governance gap

  • Symptom: partners and internal teams both compile data manually via email and spreadsheets.
  • Problem: no shared, self-serve view of transaction data exists for any layer of the ecosystem.
  • Root cause: as M2P's partner network grew, no single reporting layer was ever built to scale with it.
How might we

How might we give M2P's teams and partners a self-serve view of the same transaction data, without each side needing the other to compile it?

Opportunity Mapping

From insight to business goal

Four concrete business goals came directly out of the research: support existing partners with tailored business intelligence, support M2P's BD team in pitching new clients, act as a combination of program management and reporting, and help partners set, track, and reach their own goals.

Stage 03
Ideate

The same feature, ranked differently by who's asking

Priority Matrix

Run twice — once per audience, on purpose

Total load and decline reasons ranked high-value, high-frequency for partners watching their own program's health day to day. For M2P's internal users, tracking partner progress and decline improvement ranked highest instead — the same transaction data, prioritized around a different job to be done.

Fig 04 — Two prioritization matrices, one information architecture built to serve both
Ideation

Hierarchy over dashboards-per-audience

Alternative considered: building separate, simpler dashboards per audience. Rejected because: it would have re-created the exact silo problem the research identified — each group with its own view, no shared source of truth. The chosen direction was one hierarchy, entered from different directions by different users.

Stage 04
Prototype

Making a drill-down hierarchy feel like one screen, not five

Information Architecture

Split by outcome, not by audience

Transaction data split into Success and Decline views from the top, each breaking down by volume, channel, merchant, reason, and network — with a parallel structure for card data by status and activity. Customer Data and Card Data both needed a view-by-network and view-by-business path, because a partner and an M2P operator enter the same data from opposite directions.

Interaction Design

Hierarchy, infographics, segmentation

The finished dashboard leans on three ideas traceable directly to research: a hierarchical structure with filters visible at every level, infographics that let people stay at their preferred zoom level (trend line or raw number), and segmentation that lets the same dataset be read by network, business, or customer range.

Fig 06 — The shipped dashboard, switchable by tenant, network, and business
The takeawayA four-layer hierarchy doesn't have to feel like navigating four layers, as long as every filter is visible from wherever you currently are.
Stage 05
Test

Zero task failures still isn't the same as done

Usability Testing

A real, scored testing round — one of the few in this portfolio

Participants: 22 real users drawn from both M2P's own teams and external partners. Evaluated against: ease of use, learnability, discoverability, efficiency, and how well they could perceive the interface's elements — not just task completion.

Usability Test Findings

A four-point scale, not a pass/fail count

Outcome bandResult
Completed with ease60%
Completed, needed a little help25%
More difficult, even after explanation15%
Failed outright0%
Fig 07 — Task performance by difficulty band, and where the product goes from here
Iteration

Where the 25% and 15% point, specifically

Finding: 40% of users needed help or struggled → Problem: likely terminology and layout, not core logic, since 0% failed outright → Design change: targeted terminology and layout review for the affected sections → Expected outcome: moving more of the 25%/15% into the "completed with ease" band on a re-test.

Evidence gap

A second, post-iteration testing round to confirm this shift wasn't documented — the iteration plan is real, its outcome isn't yet measured.

Final Design

The dashboard, as tested

The hierarchical, filter-first dashboard shown in Stage 04 is the exact version that produced the 60/25/15/0 result above — not a later, unvalidated iteration.

Final Result

What actually got measured

MetricResult
Task failure rate0%
Completed with ease60%
Time on task / SUS scoreNot measured

What improved: a governed, self-serve view replacing manual reporting for three audiences at once. What didn't fully resolve: 40% of tested users still needed help or found parts difficult. What surprised me: nobody failed outright, which said more about the underlying logic being sound than about the interface being fully clear. What's next: real-time data refresh, ML-driven visuals, and a terminology pass targeting exactly the users who needed help.

Future Scope

  • Real-time streaming and data refresh, replacing the periodic refresh cycle the first version shipped with.
  • Automated machine learning and AI-driven visuals, moving from descriptive reporting toward predictive insight.
  • Integration with the SharePoint ecosystem, plus advanced data modeling and an optimized mobile app experience.

Key Learnings

Interview both ends of the relationship
M2P's internal teams and its external partners named the same gap independently — that's what confirmed it was structural, not a one-off complaint.
User groups beat a single persona for an ecosystem product
Three named groups kept every design trade-off honest about which audience it actually served.
Prioritize per audience, not once for everyone
The same transaction data ranked differently depending on whether a partner or an M2P operator was asking.
A zero-failure result is a floor, not a finish line
The 40% who needed help or struggled are a more useful signal than the 60% who sailed through.

The through-line: a dashboard serving an entire ecosystem isn't one product with one user. It's several products wearing the same interface — and the only project in this portfolio where I can prove that with a real, scored number.
