    Redesigning Pockets

    ICICI's Pockets team came in with a clear diagnosis, not a vague brief: the app was failing user trust and loyalty. This traces the audit that confirmed why, and the redesign across nine core flows that followed.

    Ayyappan Rajendiran · UX Designer, ICICI Bank
    01Overview

    Project Context

    Fig 01 — The home dashboard: the screen every other flow in this app returns to

    Pockets is ICICI Bank's real digital wallet product, and this redesign was a client requirement from the Pockets team itself, not a self-initiated exploration. The team came with a specific diagnosis already in hand: the current app wasn't meeting user expectations, and that gap was actively costing the product user trust and loyalty.     02The Problem

    Four failures, one broken trust relationship

    ICICI's Pockets team came to this project with a clear diagnosis, not a vague brief: the current app wasn't meeting user expectations, and that was actively costing the product user trust and loyalty. Four specific problem areas were named as the root causes.

    • Legacy UI — the visual language hadn't kept pace with current design patterns, making the app feel dated against newer banking and wallet products.
    • Brand inconsistency — screens didn't consistently reflect a single, coherent brand identity across the app's nine flows.
    • Usability failures — core flows created friction that worked against the basic jobs users came to the app to do.
    • Accessibility failures — the interface fell short of the accessibility standards a banking product handling real money should meet.
    Problem Statement

    Pockets isn't failing because any one screen is broken — it's failing because legacy UI, brand inconsistency, and usability and accessibility gaps are compounding into users not trusting the app enough to stay loyal to it.

    03UX Audit

    Auditing the existing app against those four dimensions

    Before any redesign work started, the existing Pockets app was audited against the four problem areas the team had named — checking where legacy UI, brand drift, usability gaps, and accessibility shortfalls actually showed up across the product, so the redesign that followed was responding to specific findings rather than a general refresh.

    Legacy UI audit

    Reviewed every flow's visual language against current interface patterns — card design, typography, iconography, and layout density.

    Brand consistency audit

    Checked whether colour, type, and component usage held together as one identity across all nine flows, or drifted flow to flow.

    Usability audit

    Walked each of the nine core flows end-to-end, flagging steps that added friction without adding clarity or confidence.

    Accessibility audit

    Checked contrast, tap-target sizing, and labelling conventions against baseline accessibility expectations for a financial product.

    The audit's findings became the brief for every decision in the four redesign stages that follow — a single reused payment pattern to close the usability gap, one consistent visual system to close the brand-consistency gap, and up-front cost transparency on the two flows that most needed users' trust.

    04Onboarding & Setup

    Selling the app before asking for a KYC document

    Onboarding opens with three benefit-led screens — hassle-free shopping, quick bill pay, scan and pay — before any account setup starts. Only after that does it move into the harder part: mobile verification, a 4-digit passcode, and KYC via PAN, Passport, Driving Licence, or Voter ID. Leading with what the app does for the user, before asking for identity documents, is a deliberate sequencing choice — the ask gets harder to justify if it comes first.

    Fig 03 — Mobile verification → 4-digit passcode → KYC (PAN/Passport/DL/Voter ID) → straight into the dashboard
    05The Dashboard

    The card is the hero; everything else is one tap away

    The dashboard puts the card balance front and centre — a virtual card rendered at near-real scale, with a masked number, expiry, and a live balance. Below it: four primary actions (Transfer, Scan QR, Bill Pay, Recharge) get one-tap top billing, while seven secondary services (Split Bills, Savings, Easy Loan, Card, Rewards, Offers, Referrals) sit one tier down in an "Other Services" grid — a deliberate two-tier hierarchy rather than flattening all eleven actions into one grid.

    Below that: a recent-transactions list, a card-activation nudge when relevant, a "link your bank accounts" cross-sell, and an offers carousel — in that order, so the account's own activity outranks promotional content.

    Fig 04 — Card and primary actions first; transactions, cross-sell, and offers follow in that order
    06Everyday Money Flows

    Four flows, one repeated pattern

    Add Money, Bill Payments, QR Code scan-to-pay, and Transfer Money all move money out of or into the same card — and all four were built on the identical sequence: enter or confirm an amount, choose a payment method (with the Pockets card pre-selected and Card/Net Banking/UPI as fallbacks), verify a 4-digit OTP, then a full-screen success confirmation that states the exact amount and counterparty. Once a user learns this pattern once, all four flows already feel familiar.

    Add Money

    Preset amounts, or type your own

    Three preset amount chips (₹1,000 / ₹2,000 / ₹5,000) sit above a free-text field, so the common case is one tap while an exact amount is still one field away.

    Fig 05 — Add Money: amount → card details → OTP → success
    Bill Payments

    Due bills surfaced above the category list

    "My Bills" — the bills already due, each with a red "Bill on due" flag and a one-tap Pay Now — sits above the general "Pay bills and subscription" category list (LPG, DTH/Cable TV, Phone). Urgency ranks above browsing.

    Fig 06 — Bill Payments: due bills first → payment method → OTP → success
    QR Code / Scan & Pay

    The merchant fills itself in

    Scanning a code auto-populates the merchant name and amount before the user does anything else — the only decision left is which payment method to confirm with.

    Fig 07 — Scan → merchant and amount auto-filled → OTP → success
    Transfer Money

    Three ways in, one shared destination

    Bank Transfer, Phone Number, and Self Transfer are offered as three entry points into the same underlying flow, with recent transactions and a searchable contacts list (Contacts / Favourites / New User) reducing the sending flow to picking a person rather than re-entering their details.

    Fig 08 — Transfer Money: method → contact → amount → success
    07Credit & Savings

    The two flows that needed more than the standard pattern

    Easy Loan and Savings Plan both move real money like the four flows above, but both needed an extra decision layer before the standard pattern could apply — so both get a dedicated "details" screen the other four don't need.

    Easy Loan

    Showing the real cost before asking for a decision

    Loan type (Personal / Two-Wheeler) and a tenure selector (3/6/12/24 months) each show their own EMI live as they're adjusted, then a full breakdown — EMI, total of all EMIs, annual interest rate, total interest, processing fee, GST, and net transfer amount — renders before the "Transfer Funds" button is ever shown. The cost is fully visible before commitment, not after.

    Fig 09 — Easy Loan: type + tenure → full EMI/interest/fee breakdown → success
    Savings Plan

    Interest rate as the deciding variable, made visible per tenure

    Fixed Deposit and Recurring Deposit share one flow. As the tenure changes (3/6/12/24 months), the interest rate updates alongside it (6.0% → 7.0%) — the variable that actually drives the decision is shown at the moment it's being made, not buried in a separate terms screen.

    Fig 10 — Savings Plan: FD/RD → tenure with live interest rate → payment method → success
    08Key Learnings

    Key Learnings

    One pattern beats nine bespoke ones
    Reusing amount → method → OTP → confirmation across Add Money, Bill Pay, QR, and Transfer made four unrelated jobs feel like one app, not four features stapled together.
    Show the cost before the commitment
    Easy Loan and Savings Plan both surface their real numbers — EMI/interest or interest rate — before asking for a final action, rather than after.
    Audit first, redesign second
    Naming legacy UI, brand inconsistency, usability, and accessibility as four distinct audit dimensions — before touching a single screen — gave every redesign decision a specific problem to answer, not a general "make it better" brief.
